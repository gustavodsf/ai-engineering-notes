# How Generative and Agentic AI Shift Concern from Technical Debt to Cognitive Debt

Source: https://margaretstorey.com/blog/2026/02/09/cognitive-debt/  
Author: Margaret-Anne Storey

Verified: HTTP 200, full text read on 27 November 2026

## Key points

- Technical debt lives in the code; cognitive debt lives in developers' minds and affects their ability to "go fast" or change things. Even if agents produce readable code, people may have "lost the plot".
- Cognitive debt may be a bigger threat than technical debt as agents are adopted. Peter Naur: a program is a theory in the minds of its developers.
- The student-team story: by weeks 7–8 they couldn't make simple changes; the problem was not messy code but missing shared understanding.
- Practices: require that at least one human fully understands each AI-generated change before it ships, document not just what changed but why, and hold regular checkpoints (reviews, retrospectives, knowledge sharing). Pair programming, refactoring and TDD help against both technical and cognitive debt.
- Warning signs: hesitation to change code, tribal knowledge held by one or two people, the system feeling like a black box.
