## Key points

- Fowler summarises Margaret-Anne Storey's three layers of system health: technical debt lives in code, cognitive debt lives in people, intent debt lives in artifacts. He is "a bit bemused by debt metaphor proliferation" but says the framing "does make a fair bit of sense", and that the article has useful diagnosis and mitigation sections.
- Cites Shaw and Nave (Wharton) on AI as "System 3" and "cognitive surrender", uncritical reliance on AI reasoning, as distinct from cognitive offloading.
- Quotes Ajey Gore: if coding agents make coding free, verification becomes the expensive part; the human job becomes designing verification systems and defining quality. Fowler agrees with most of it, but says LLMs help a great deal in understanding what legacy code is doing.
